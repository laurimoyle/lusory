A field guide

Lusory

A shelf of games. Each one asks you to do something the hard way, on purpose, for no profit anyone could bank.

A philosopher called this "the voluntary attempt to overcome unnecessary obstacles" — his definition of a game. We mean it as a way to spend the spare corners of a life.

There are no streaks here, no scores, no history. Leave for a month and nothing will have noticed, and everything will be where you left it.

One date will be kept on this device — the day you last opened this. Nothing else, ever.

The old story

The old story, in five lines

All summer the grasshopper sang while the ant hauled grain. Winter came. The grasshopper, starving, asked the ant for a little food. “What did you do all summer?” “I sang.” “Then dance,” said the ant, and shut the door.

Aesop’s moral: work first, sing later — if ever.

That grasshopper lives here.

The fable’s loser is this shelf’s one character — kept on because the whole app takes his side of the case.

"That’s the story. Aesop reported the ending and missed the point. Nobody ever asks about the summer."

About

What this is

Games, in the old strict sense: a goal, some rules that forbid the easy way, and your free agreement to play anyway. The philosopher Bernard Suits argued that this — not usefulness — is what a good life is made of. Josef Pieper said something near it about leisure: useless on purpose, and formative because of it.

About the science, briefly

These games draw on, and adapt, practices with peer-reviewed support — the research behind The Slow Taste, The Small Self, Small Kindnesses, Opening Up, The Moving Body, Talking to Strangers and The Long Exhale, among others. The honest summary of that research: effects are real and mostly small to moderate. This app's contribution isn't bigger effects; it's a form people freely return to.

One finding shaped the whole design: measuring an enjoyable activity tends to make it feel like work. So nothing you do here is counted, stored, ranked, or shown back to you — and no claim about this app will ever be built from watching you use it.

This is play, preventative and formative — not treatment. If you're in real difficulty, a clinician beats a game, and we'd rather you had the clinician.

Privacy

No account, no cloud, no analytics, and nothing sent anywhere. One date is kept on this device once you have played a game: the day you last opened this. Nothing else, ever. Your reflections stay in the page and evaporate when you close it.
